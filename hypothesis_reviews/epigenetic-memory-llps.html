Epigenetic Memory: LLPS vs. Enzymatic Propagation

Histone-mark recovery and selected heterochromatin assembly measurements; these do not establish inheritance of a liquid phase.

Earlier text appraisal: 3 October 2026 Separate archived document available CellNucleus Hypothesis Review Series
Scientific text and primary references rechecked · 12 October 2026. Scientific factual paragraphs link to primary studies. A measured mark-transmission result at replication

Alabert and colleagues used nascent chromatin capture, isotope labeling and mass spectrometry in cultured human cells to distinguish parental and newly synthesized histones. Modified parental histones were recovered on replicated DNA, while new histones initially diluted modification levels. This is biochemical transmission and recovery evidence at the population level. It is not a genomic-address map, a single-nucleosome neighbor-copying movie or a test showing that a liquid compartment survives cell division. Alabert et al., 2015.

Total recovery differs from immediate copying

Many newly deposited histone profiles approached parental profiles during maturation, whereas H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 accumulated progressively on both old and new histones. Total recovery can therefore occur without every new histone immediately matching a marked parent. The result constrains a simple instantaneous copying story, but does not rank all enzymatic, spatial and phase models of cellular memory. Persistence of mark abundance is also distinct from persistence of a measured cell phenotype. Alabert et al., 2015.

An H1 perturbation adds regional information

Liu and colleagues followed H3K27me3 recovery after replication in mouse embryonic stem cells using ChOR-seq. Recovery varied across genomic regions and was delayed by selected H1 depletion in chronic and inducible comparisons. This supports an H1 contribution in that system. Other H1 variants remained, and the readout does not directly track nucleosome contacts or isolate physical compaction from every H1 function. It neither tests liquid-phase inheritance nor demonstrates that mark recovery alone preserves cell identity. Liu et al., 2023.

HP1 assembly capacity differs from the tested cellular state

Erdel and colleagues observed purified mouse HP1α droplets with DNA under the examined in-vitro conditions. In mouse fibroblasts, chromocenters persisted after Suv39h1/2 loss removed strong H3K9me3/HP1 enrichment. In a separate engineered U2OS reporter system, tethered HP1 repressed expression without a detected droplet assembly. These comparisons show why enrichment, repression and purified droplet capacity cannot be equated with phase-based memory. They are not complete HP1 deletion or multi-generation natural-locus inheritance experiments, and they do not exclude every phase process in other heterochromatin systems. Erdel et al., 2020.

A transmission mechanism with a silencing phenotype — 12 October 2026

Charlton and colleagues connected parental histone handling to inheritance of a measured silencing state in fission yeast. Mrc1 connector-domain and Mcm2 histone-binding mutants disrupted lagging-strand recycling and produced asymmetric loss of H3K9me-associated reporter silencing in daughter lineages. Their biochemical and genetic comparisons support a recycling mechanism in that organism. This supplies a cellular inheritance readout beyond total mark recovery; it does not establish human transgenerational inheritance or a requirement for a liquid chromatin compartment. Charlton et al. (2024).
