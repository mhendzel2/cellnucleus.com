Genome Organization Hypotheses Locus- and time-dependent effects

3D Genome Causality: Structure vs. Function

What targeted boundary rearrangements and acute architectural-protein depletion can establish about gene regulation

Earlier text appraisal: 3 October 2026 Separate archived document available CellNucleus Hypothesis Review Series
Scientific text and primary references rechecked · 12 October 2026. Scientific factual paragraphs link to primary studies. The independent audit records reference identity, claim fit and access depth, including abstract-only and selected-passage limits. Linked source documents and raw data remain separate work. See audit scope and evidence.

1. Define the causal claim

A structural contribution to regulation means that an intervention changes a specified architectural feature and a relevant expression outcome. It is stronger evidence than contact and expression merely covarying. Two useful tests are rearranging a regulatory boundary at a developmental locus and acutely removing architectural proteins while measuring both contacts and nascent transcription. Neither test automatically answers whether structure always precedes function throughout the genome. [1] [2]

2. A developmental locus where boundary context matters

Lupiáñez and colleagues investigated human limb-malformation variants around WNT6, IHH, EPHA4 and PAX3 and engineered corresponding mouse rearrangements. Mouse embryonic limb 4C profiles and expression measurements showed ectopic contacts and activation of neighboring genes by the Epha4 regulatory neighborhood. Comparisons with deletions that spared the boundary region retained normal rather than ectopic expression of the relevant neighboring genes. This supports a causal contribution of boundary-containing sequence at this locus. [1]

The rearrangements change genomic neighborhood and sequence as well as measured contacts. They therefore do not isolate physical geometry as the sole cause. Patient fibroblast contact profiles also cannot substitute for a direct expression measurement in human embryonic limbs. The primary abstract and figure legends were accessible here; full methods and supplementary analysis were not appraised. [1]

3. Structural disruption need not immediately erase expression

Hsieh and colleagues depleted CTCF, RAD21 or WAPL for three hours in mouse embryonic stem cells, combining Micro-C with nascent and total RNA profiling. Removing CTCF or RAD21 strongly disrupted structural-loop features, while most enhancer–promoter contacts and gene expression remained largely maintained. The result includes a weak overall reduction in enhancer–promoter contacts and a responsive subset; it is not a claim of zero effect. [2]

This tests short-term maintenance in an existing stem-cell state. It leaves open roles in establishing regulatory contacts, during differentiation, or after longer depletion. The authors' proposed time-buffering or molecular-memory explanation is a model, not a mechanism proven by these measurements. Moreover, a focal contact signal does not require a permanently loop-shaped enhancer–promoter configuration. [2]

4. Assessment: avoid a universal causal arrow

These results can coexist: a particular developmental boundary can restrain inappropriate enhancer action, while many established contacts and transcripts persist during short architectural-factor depletion. They support locus- and time-dependent architectural effects. They do not establish a genome-wide structure-first, transcription-first or inevitable bidirectional feedback mechanism. A causal statement should name the locus, intervention, time window and expression readout that support it. [1] [2]

Regulatory activation can also shape a contact pattern — 12 October 2026

In zebrafish embryos, Galitsyna and colleagues reported enhancer-associated Hi-C “fountains” during zygotic genome activation. Removing pioneer transcription factors reduced these patterns, linking an enhancer-activation perturbation to architectural change in that setting. A proposed mechanism involving facilitated cohesin loading and desynchronized extrusion came from polymer simulations. This adds evidence for regulatory activity influencing folding, while preserving the distinction between the measured contact pattern and its mechanistic model. Galitsyna et al. (2026).
