Nuclear Condensates: Quality Control or Functional Regulators?

Separate transcriptional function, physical mechanism, and protein quality control when interpreting nuclear condensate experiments.

Updated 3 October 2026 Concise overview with linked primary sources CellNucleus Hypothesis Review Series
Scientific audit: partial · 3 October 2026. Selected claims or references checked; this page has not received a full scientific review. See checked scope and remaining work.

Review scope — partial audit

Targeted scientific correction checked 3 October 2026. This concise overview checks selected LLPS mechanisms, assay interpretation, and the primary studies linked below. It is not a systematic review or validation of every associated document, figure label, or historical citation. Downloadable source documents remain outside this correction.

Function and quality control

Enrichment of transcriptional machinery and quality control are distinct questions that may coexist. Functional evidence requires showing how an assembly changes a biological output; demonstrating LLPS requires physical evidence for demixing. One does not automatically establish the other. Evidence and interpretation: Boija et al. (2018); Cho et al. (2018); Itoh et al. (2021); Sahin et al. (2014).

The original attribution is corrected: Boija et al. (2018) was published in Cell, whereas Cho et al. (2018) appeared in Science. Neither study establishes that all super-enhancer transcription depends on a universal liquid compartment.

Transcription-linked evidence

Boija et al. (2018) reported that mutations of OCT4 and GCN4 activation-domain residues affected both in vitro co-condensation with Mediator and gene activation. This supports a connection; changing interaction residues can also change ordinary coactivator binding, limiting a phase-specific causal interpretation.

Cho et al. (2018) imaged endogenous Mediator and Pol II clusters in mouse embryonic stem cells, including their association with active genes and responses to JQ1 and transcription inhibition. The experiment supports regulated clustering in that context.

Perturbation limits

JQ1 disrupts BET bromodomain-dependent chromatin recruitment, so loss of clusters and transcription is not a selective test of LLPS. The relevant issue is the drug’s known biological target and its downstream effects, rather than a blanket claim of unspecified off-target activity. FRAP and morphology also need complementary mechanism tests. Evidence and interpretation: Cho et al. (2018).

Itoh et al. (2021) found that hexanediol treatment affects chromatin motion and condensation. Transcription changes after this treatment cannot be attributed exclusively to droplet dissolution.

Quality-control evidence has its own scope

PML-associated protein processing provides a concrete example: Sahin et al. (2014) linked stress-induced body assembly to partner SUMOylation and, for some partners, RNF4-associated ubiquitination and degradation. This does not establish that Mediator/BRD4 hubs exist mainly to sequester misfolded proteins.

An experiment separating these hypotheses would monitor nascent transcription and protein-quality-control outputs while controlling scaffold abundance, binding, localization, and cell stress. The proposed “condensates mainly as quality control” explanation remains a hypothesis for transcription hubs. Evidence and interpretation: Sahin et al. (2014).

Evidence assessment

Regulated assemblies are observed; general functional necessity and material mechanism remain separate tests. The evidence cited here supports links between selected interaction domains, clustering, and transcription. It does not justify either a universal LLPS requirement or a universal quality-control explanation. Evidence and interpretation: Boija et al. (2018); Cho et al. (2018); Itoh et al. (2021); Sahin et al. (2014).

Engineered material changes test transcription output

Evidence update · 12 October 2026

A 2024 optogenetic study systematically varied transcription-factor assemblies and found different gene-output responses associated with their material behavior in mammalian cells and mice. This adds an engineered functional comparison; it does not establish that endogenous transcription hubs serve primarily as misfolded-protein reservoirs. Engineering Material Properties of Transcription Factor Condensates to Control Gene Expression in Mammalian Cells and Mice (2024).

Conceptual model and measured examples

The former diagram combined chaperone, disaggregase and aggregate actors whose specific roles were not established by this page’s evidence set, so that scientific illustration has been withdrawn from this edition. PML-associated partner processing and transcription-linked clustering remain separately sourced observations. Oxidative stress-induced assembly of PML nuclear bodies controls sumoylation of partner proteins (2014); Mediator and RNA polymerase II clusters associate in transcription-dependent condensates (2018).

In a different, specifically reconstituted system, FUS droplets converted toward an aggregated state and patient-associated variants accelerated conversion. This example supports a protein-specific material transition; it does not assign the same transition to every transcription hub.
