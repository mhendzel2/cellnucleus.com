Genome Organization Hypotheses Measured, context-dependent organization

Chromosome Territory Architecture Mechanisms

Chromosome paints reveal probabilistic positions; contact maps and live tracking measure different features of nuclear organization.

What a chromosome territory measurement establishes

A territory is an operationally resolved chromosome-rich region in these DNA-paint images. In fixed human male fibroblasts, multicolor 3D FISH mapped the relative positions of individual chromosomes. Most chromosome territories in the 54 quiescent nuclei could be classified, but a painted optical boundary does not establish a physical wall or complete absence of intermingling. Bolzer et al., 2005.

Radial position depends on the sample and geometric metric

The flat fibroblast nuclei showed a chromosome-size-related tendency: smaller chromosomes were more central and larger chromosomes more peripheral. The principal measurements did not find a significant chromosome 18/19 separation. Consequently, the familiar gene-density explanation cannot be turned into a universal address rule for every human cell. Nearest-envelope distance and distance from a nuclear center also answer different geometric questions. Bolzer et al., 2005.

Probabilistic neighbors and a different yeast measurement

Individual fibroblast nuclei had variable chromosome-neighbor arrangements rather than one repeated map. In budding yeast, Duan and colleagues instead measured population proximity-ligation contacts: centromeres showed enriched contacts and chromosome-arm organization was consistent with a Rabl arrangement. These results describe distinct readouts. A frequent contact in a population does not tell us that two territories touch continuously in every cell. Bolzer et al., 2005; Duan et al., 2010.

What these studies leave open

The evidence supports nonrandom, context-dependent organization without specifying a single fixed chromosome map. The fibroblast study compared chromosome geometry and repeat-density patterns; its Alu signal is not a transcription assay. The yeast 3D reconstruction depends on assumptions connecting contact frequency to distance and on nuclear landmarks. Neither experiment separately changes chromosome position while holding regulatory sequence and cell state constant, so neither proves that a radial position by itself determines gene expression. Bolzer et al., 2005; Duan et al., 2010.

A tissue example of cell-type-dependent organization — 12 October 2026

Genome-scale DNA/RNA imaging by Liu and colleagues found different radial and higher-order genome organization across identified cell types in mouse primary motor cortex. This adds a tissue-based counterpoint to the fixed human fibroblast and yeast measurements above. It does not establish a universal chromosome address or isolate radial position alone as the cause of every expression difference. Liu et al. (2025).
