Chromatin Architecture & Dynamics — Integrative Review

Histone incorporation, locus motion and developmental ultrastructure provide distinct, context-dependent measurements of chromatin change.

Dynamics requires a specified observable

“Chromatin dynamics” can describe changes in histone composition, the distance between labeled genomic anchors, or the distribution of compact material in a cell population. These are different measurements. Deal and colleagues followed new histone incorporation in Drosophila cells; Gabriele and colleagues tracked one mouse genomic domain in living cells; Ahmed and colleagues compared fixed mouse embryonic cell states. Agreement that chromatin changes does not make their spatial resolutions, clocks or biological causes interchangeable. Deal et al., 2010; Gabriele et al., 2022; Ahmed et al., 2010.

Histone incorporation is heterogeneous across the genome

In asynchronous Drosophila S2 cells, CATCH-IT captured newly made histones and mapped associated DNA. Active gene bodies and selected regulatory-factor binding sites showed comparatively high H3/H4 incorporation, whereas Polycomb-associated sites showed lower but measurable turnover. Pulse/chase behavior supported replacement rather than simply reading a static histone-variant enrichment. This assay follows incorporation and loss of labeled histones, not the path of an intact nucleosome sliding along DNA. Deal et al., 2010.

Live locus motion and inferred loop states

Gabriele and colleagues studied a single 505 kb Fbn2 domain in engineered mouse embryonic stem cells, restricted to G1/early S, where Fbn2 was silent. Labeled boundary-anchor distances fluctuated, and a Bayesian model inferred infrequent, transient fully boundary-bridged loop states. Micro-C and CTCF/cohesin-related perturbations supplied additional structural evidence. These trajectories do not directly film the extrusion motor, and the inferred loop-state fractions and durations cannot be assigned to every genomic domain. Gabriele et al., 2022.

Ultrastructure changes across developmental states

In correlative fluorescence/ESI sections of mouse embryos, Ahmed and colleagues found more dispersed chromatin in blastocyst epiblast than in lineage-restricted cells, and more compact domains in later epiblast. Oct4-null putative epiblast also showed increased compaction. Because the images come from different fixed specimens, this is a developmental-state comparison rather than a continuous single-cell trajectory. Oct4 deletion changes the regulatory program as well as structure; it does not isolate compaction as the cause of lost pluripotency. Ahmed et al., 2010.

Expanding the live-locus sample — 12 October 2026

Sabaté and colleagues extended live anchor tracking to three endogenous TADs in human HCT116 cells, alongside Capture Micro-C, cohesin perturbation and polymer modeling. The sampled domains showed dynamic anchor proximity and were interpreted as being continuously extruded by multiple cohesin complexes. This extends the earlier single-locus evidence, while retaining locus-label, cell-line and model limits; the paper’s inferred uniform cohesin dynamics across its sampled regions is not a measured universal constant for every tissue or locus. Sabaté et al. (2025).
