Liquid-Like Assembly in the DNA Damage Response

A hypothesis assessment separating observed behavior from mechanism and repair function

HTML appraisal: 3 October 2026 Associated documents separately unvalidated CellNucleus Hypothesis Review Series
Earlier text/reference appraisal · 3 October 2026. Every retained major scientific assertion and cited reference in this revised HTML was appraised at the source access depth recorded in the audit. Source documents and other package checks remain incomplete. See scope, source access and limits.

Checked scientific scope

This revised HTML is a concise primary-evidence synthesis of descriptive DNA repair biology. Every retained scientific paragraph and reference was appraised at the stated access depth. Detailed Methods, raw data, an exhaustive literature search and the associated source document are outside this review.

The bounded hypothesis

The hypothesis considered here is that PAR-associated or 53BP1-associated assemblies can contribute liquid-like organization at damaged chromatin. It concerns particular proteins and experimental settings; it is not a claim that every DNA repair focus is one phase-separated liquid. [altmeyer2015] [kilic2019]

Evidence from PAR-associated proteins

Altmeyer and colleagues observed transient PAR-dependent FUS, EWS and TAF15 recruitment at locally damaged regions in human U-2-OS cells. They also observed concentration-associated droplet formation and fusion/fission by expressed isolated prion-like domains. [altmeyer2015]

The study links PAR-sensing RGG-rich regions with recruitment and self-associating domains with amplified assembly. The authors interpreted the combination as PAR-seeded liquid demixing. Endogenous-protein recruitment strengthens relevance, while isolated-domain droplets and local laser injury limit extrapolation to all physiological repair compartments. [altmeyer2015]

Evidence from 53BP1

Kilic and colleagues reported droplet-like fusion and fission of 53BP1-marked compartments using live microscopy and endogenous tagging. Their Abstract reports sensitivity of 53BP1 assembly to physical and chemical conditions while gamma-H2AX and MDC1 accumulation were less affected. [kilic2019]

Artificial 53BP1 optoDroplets enriched p53, and disruption-associated conditions impaired the reported p53 response. This adds a signaling endpoint to the assembly observations, but artificial clustering and chemical sensitivity do not uniquely diagnose endogenous LLPS or demonstrate that every repair reaction requires it. [kilic2019]

What the observations distinguish

Recruitment, fusion/fission, optical behavior and downstream signaling measure different aspects of a response. Collectively they support liquid-like behavior in the reported assemblies. They leave room for specific binding and chromatin-associated interactions to participate in the same compartment. [altmeyer2015] [kilic2019]

A dependence of a recruitment or signaling readout on a perturbation is not the same as selective dependence of completed DNA repair on phase separation. Neither study, as appraised at the stated depth here, establishes an exclusive phase mechanism for all endogenous DNA damage foci. [altmeyer2015] [kilic2019]

Assessment and access limits

The supported assessment is protein- and context-specific: PAR-associated FET proteins and 53BP1 compartments show evidence consistent with liquid-like assembly, while universal necessity for DNA repair is unresolved by this evidence set. The Altmeyer main Results and selected legends were inspected; Kilic was inspected at primary Abstract and selected figure-caption depth. [altmeyer2015] [kilic2019]

A 2026 regulator connects PAR turnover with 53BP1 assembly

Evidence update · 12 October 2026

Heo and colleagues report that RSF1 affects PARG recruitment, PAR persistence, 53BP1 optoDroplet assembly and p53-target transcription. This adds a temporally regulated molecular connection; the assembly and signaling readouts do not by themselves establish that every completed endogenous repair event selectively requires liquid-phase separation. RSF1-Dependent PAR Turnover Promotes 53BP1 Liquid Condensate Formation at DNA Damage Sites (2026).
