Intake analysis

What the figure batch can support

The collection is strongest as an interpretive teaching set. It repeatedly shows segmented or panel-based FIB-SEM views of nuclei from HeLa, Jurkat, macrophage, COS-7, kidney, liver, and immune-contact examples. These exports use darkfield-style contrast: bright structures sit on black backgrounds. The page below uses visible patterns and filename context, but formal figure legends should be checked against the original OpenOrganelle dataset metadata before publication.

Guided readouts

Different aspects of nuclear ultrastructure

Envelope and periphery

The nucleus as a compartment, not a flat outline

FIB-SEM sections place the nuclear boundary inside the surrounding cytoplasm, making the envelope, adjacent organelles, and peripheral chromatin context easier to discuss together. This is useful for explaining why nuclear architecture is both a genome-organization problem and a cell-geometry problem.

Related nuclear envelope review

Chromatin texture

Dense and open nuclear material at nanometer scale

The high-magnification panels show heterogeneous electron density inside the nucleus. Used carefully, these views help introduce the visual difference between compacted material, less-dense regions, and the problem of mapping EM contrast onto molecular chromatin states.

Related heterochromatin review

Nucleolus

A nuclear body visible without fluorescence labels

Nucleolus-labeled examples from different cell contexts give the page a direct route into ribosome-biogenesis compartments, nuclear body organization, and the limits of identifying compartments from morphology alone.

Related nucleolus review

Nuclear bodies

Compartment boundaries can be inferred, segmented, and debated

The macrophage examples are useful for showing how the same region can be presented as raw slices or as a more segmented visual. That contrast supports a stronger teaching point: nuclear body interpretation depends on imaging, annotation, and biological context.

Cell-type variation

Nuclear ultrastructure changes with cellular context

The selected batch includes immune cells, epithelial-like cells, tissue sections, and infection or contact contexts. The safest public framing is comparative: the images show that nuclear shape, surrounding cytoplasm, and internal density patterns vary across preparations.

Individual image-to-dataset provenance, molecular annotations, and reuse licenses remain unresolved.
