Two measured cluster populations in mouse stem cells

Cho and colleagues imaged endogenously tagged Mediator and RNA polymerase II in mouse embryonic stem cells. Super-resolution imaging resolved small transient clusters and larger clusters that persisted through the acquisition window. The latter often colocalized across Mediator and Pol II channels. During differentiation, large-cluster size and number decreased. “Stable” here means stable over the observation period, rather than a permanent transcription factory present in every cell type. Cho et al., 2018.

Transcriptional perturbations affect the components differently

In the same study, JQ1 treatment disrupted both Mediator and Pol II clusters. With the transcription-associated inhibitor DRB, stable Pol II clusters dissolved while stable Mediator clusters remained, and their colocalization recovered after washout. This supports distinct dependencies for the two assemblies. Because these interventions alter recruitment and transcriptional activity, the resulting cluster changes do not independently establish that a liquid phase is required for transcription. Cho et al., 2018.

Exchange, merging and selected-locus proximity

Light-sheet imaging documented cluster merging, and photobleaching experiments showed molecular exchange. Esrrb RNA labeling and Mediator tracking in six cells showed intermittent close proximity rather than continuous overlap. The authors proposed a dynamic interaction model. The optical proximity threshold does not demonstrate direct molecular contact, and FRAP recovery measures exchange rather than uniquely identifying a liquid-liquid phase transition. These observations support dynamic organization while leaving the microscopic mechanism open. Cho et al., 2018.

Activation domains provide complementary interaction evidence

Boija and colleagues found that purified MED1 disordered-region droplets enriched OCT4 relative to a GFP control. OCT4 activation-domain acidic-residue mutations reduced incorporation into droplets and reduced activation of an engineered GAL4 reporter in mouse stem cells. This links the same interaction chemistry to partitioning and activation. It does not separate phase behavior from ordinary coactivator interactions, and it does not demonstrate that every Pol II transcription event requires a condensate. Boija et al., 2018.
