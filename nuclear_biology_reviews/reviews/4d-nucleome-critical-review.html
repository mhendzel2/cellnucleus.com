1. A contact map is an ensemble measurement

The original Hi-C study mapped contacts in human GM06990 lymphoblastoid and K562 leukemia cells. Contacts within a chromosome were enriched, and the maps contained A/B compartments associated with accessibility and transcriptional features. Selected DNA FISH measurements provided an independent spatial comparison. These findings support organized chromosome folding; they do not make every A region uniformly active or every B region uniformly silent. [1]

Hi-C frequencies summarize many fixed cells. They report how often sequences are recovered together, rather than one uniquely determined three-dimensional genome or a movie of a chromosome. Differences between the two cell types therefore identify associations with cellular state, not proof that compartment changes caused their expression differences. [1]

2. A measured transition: mitosis to G1

Zhang and colleagues sampled highly purified, synchronous mouse erythroid populations after mitosis. Their Hi-C time course found rapid appearance of A/B compartments followed by strengthening and expansion. Smaller contact domains appeared before merging into larger domains. CTCF binding recovered earlier than focal cohesin binding, and structural-loop formation tracked cohesin positioning. The result does not support a blanket claim that all TADs precede compartments. [2]

This is temporal ordering among sampled populations, not repeated observation of each individual cell. The accessible abstract and extended-data descriptions support these qualitative results; the full subscription article was not read in this audit. The timing should not be extended to every developmental transition or used alone to infer that folding changes switch transcription on. [2]

3. Live tracking resolves a specific locus

Gabriele and colleagues tracked labeled anchors of one 505-kb Fbn2 domain in mouse embryonic stem cells, restricting analysis to G1 and early S phase. Anchor distances changed over time. Micro-C controls and CTCF, cohesin and WAPL perturbations linked those distances to architectural factors. Bayesian analysis inferred infrequent, transient fully bridged boundary loops at this locus. [3]

The images follow fluorescent locus labels, not a cohesin motor threading DNA. Loop-state assignments require assumptions about noisy distance trajectories. Fbn2 is silent in these cells, so the experiment does not directly test an enhancer–promoter loop driving its transcription. A lifetime inferred here is not a lifetime for every TAD or every cell type. [3]

4. What the fourth dimension establishes

Together, these studies distinguish population folding patterns, their recovery during a defined cell-cycle transition, and fluctuating distances within living cells. They provide concrete evidence that genome organization is dynamic. They leave separate questions about which changes cause a regulatory output, how much variation exists across loci and tissues, and which molecular mechanism generates a given contact. This review consequently reports the measured systems and inference limits instead of a universal folding schedule. [1] [2] [3]
