Endogenous HDAC3 activity depends on its corepressor context

You et al. examined mice with altered deacetylase-activation domains in both NCoR and SMRT. HDAC3 protein levels persisted, but its assayed activity was undetectable in the tested liver, heart, skeletal muscle and embryonic samples. This supports a requirement for these corepressor domains for most measurable endogenous HDAC3 activity in the examined tissues. It does not prove that absolutely no residual activity remained below assay background. [1]

Chromatin recruitment and acetylation change together

In mutant liver, HDAC3 chromatin occupancy was reduced but not eliminated. H3K9 and H3K27 acetylation increased at selected sites where HDAC3 normally bound, while the measured corepressor recruitment remained similar. Because the perturbation changed both recruitment and assayed activity, the acetylation result should not be presented as an isolated catalytic effect with every other chromatin interaction held constant. [1]

Loss of assayed activity differs from loss of the protein

These corepressor-domain mutant mice survived to adulthood and showed more modest liver lipid and gene-expression changes than mice lacking hepatic HDAC3 protein. The authors interpreted the differences as evidence for DAD-independent or nonenzymatic HDAC3 functions, while retaining the residual-activity caveat. A protein-loss phenotype therefore cannot automatically be equated with the consequences of suppressing a catalytic assay readout. [1]

A lung-macrophage study gives a distinct cellular outcome

Yao et al. found that HDAC3 loss impaired the development and postnatal maintenance of mouse alveolar macrophages. Their expression analyses showed reduced Pparg and associated PPAR-γ pathway genes, while chromatin immunoprecipitation placed HDAC3 at Pparg-associated enhancer regions. This is a context in which loss of an HDAC accompanies reduced expression of an important lineage regulator, rather than uniform gene activation. [2]

The paper also found different consequences among macrophage subpopulations and tissue contexts. The lung result should therefore retain its cell type and developmental stage. It does not establish that every macrophage or human tissue requires the same HDAC3 program, nor does it validate a treatment derived from the mouse phenotype. [2]

A bounded conclusion about deacetylase biology

The checked studies distinguish protein abundance, corepressor association, genomic recruitment, measured enzymatic activity, histone acetylation and gene expression. They show why HDAC3 should be interpreted within its tissue and complex context. Their results do not constitute a complete catalogue of human deacetylases or demonstrate that all family members are interchangeable universal transcriptional repressors. [1] [2]
