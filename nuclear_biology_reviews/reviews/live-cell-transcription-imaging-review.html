1. What the live RNA signal measures

Liu and colleagues imaged a transgenic hunchback reporter in developing Drosophila embryos. Two colors labeled nascent RNA at different positions along the reporter, allowing the fluorescence time traces to carry information about RNA production and release. This follows an engineered reporter in living cells, rather than every step of an unmodified endogenous human gene. Fluorescence at the locus combines contributions from multiple transcripts at different stages. [1]

2. Rates and correlations are inferred, not read directly from brightness

A Bayesian model separated effective initiation, elongation and cleavage parameters from the two-color traces. The main analysis covered 355 cells from seven embryos during nuclear cycle 14, using the initial 18-minute window after mitosis. Independent reporter measurements supported the color calibration. Single-cell inference identified a negative association between estimated transcript density and cleavage time, suggesting a coupling to investigate rather than establishing its molecular cause. [1]

The model assumes a simplified transcription cycle, including uniform effective elongation and successful progression of the transcripts it measures. It explicitly does not capture strong transcriptional bursting, and highly variable traces were excluded from the fitted dataset. The findings cannot justify a claim that this analysis measures every ON/OFF transition or that all genes use one two-state mechanism. [1]

3. RNA output and chromatin distance are different observables

Live chromosome imaging answers a different question. Gabriele and colleagues tracked anchors of one Fbn2 domain in mouse embryonic stem cells and inferred changing loop states from distances. Fbn2 was silent, so those trajectories do not directly test transcription bursts or enhancer-driven RNA production. Likewise, a nascent RNA movie alone does not demonstrate that an enhancer is physically touching its promoter. Linking the two requires simultaneous or complementary measurements at the relevant regulatory locus. [2] [1]

4. Perturbation and population profiling provide complementary tests

Hsieh and colleagues combined Micro-C with nascent and total RNA profiling after three-hour depletion of architectural proteins in mouse stem cells. Most enhancer–promoter contacts and expression persisted despite substantial structural-loop disruption after CTCF or RAD21 loss; weak overall contact changes and sensitive subsets remained. This is an intervention with population readouts, not live tracking of individual transcription events. It tests short-term maintenance of an established state, while leaving establishment and later responses open. [3]

5. Assessment: match the conclusion to the readout

Live RNA imaging resolves time-dependent reporter signals, model fitting estimates effective transcription parameters, and locus tracking resolves distances. Each adds useful information while retaining its own assumptions and system limits. These experiments do not by themselves establish a universal transcription factory, condensate or stable enhancer-loop mechanism. This review keeps the measured examples and excludes those broader mechanistic claims. [1] [2] [3]
