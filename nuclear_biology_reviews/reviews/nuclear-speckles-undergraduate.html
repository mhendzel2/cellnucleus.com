Nuclear Speckles

Primary experiments distinguish speckle assembly, gene association and RNA-processing effects in specific cultured-cell systems.

What a speckle is, and how assembly was tested

Nuclear speckles are bodies without an enclosing membrane, enriched in RNA-processing proteins. Ilik and colleagues combined SRRM2 truncation with SON knockdown in human HAP1 cells, and combined SON/SRRM2 knockdown in HEK293 cells. Speckle-associated proteins became broadly dispersed, supporting a structural role for these two core proteins in those models. [1]

The same study showed that the commonly used SC35 monoclonal antibody primarily marks SRRM2 in speckles, rather than its historically assigned target SRSF2. Marker identity matters: an antibody signal identifies its target distribution, not every function of the body it labels. [1]

Gene association is a specific measurement

Kim and colleagues studied heat-shock genes in human HAP1 and hamster CHOK1 cells, including engineered HSPA1B reporters. RNA FISH and live imaging associated speckle proximity with more RNA. The timing supported an effect following association, but increased RNA can reflect altered degradation as well as transcription. These Hsp70 genes are intronless, so this result alone does not demonstrate improved splicing. [2]

A 2026 perturbation tests RNA processing

Małszycki and colleagues reported that acute removal of SON and SRRM2 in engineered human cells impaired splicing and reduced expression of GC-rich genes with short introns in GC-rich genomic regions. Their abstract reports that genes outside those regions were spared. This supports a restricted processing role in the tested system; it is not a claim that every gene requires speckles. [3]

What these experiments leave to resolve

Core-protein depletion changes both protein availability and speckle organization. Assigning each RNA phenotype specifically to spatial organization therefore needs controls that separate those effects. Neither a stained focus nor one locus-specific RNA response establishes a universal liquid material state or disease mechanism. [1] [2] [3]
