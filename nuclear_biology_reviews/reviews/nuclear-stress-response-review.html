A ribosomal protein can regulate p53 turnover

Zhang and colleagues reported binding of ribosomal protein L11 to human MDM2. This association limited MDM2-mediated p53 ubiquitination and degradation and supported p53-linked transcription and cell-cycle-arrest endpoints. [zhang2003]

Impaired ribosome biogenesis was associated with increased L11-MDM2 interaction and p53 stabilization. The authors proposed a ribosomal-integrity checkpoint, bounded here to the interaction and cellular endpoints reported in their primary Abstract. [zhang2003]

The signaling entity includes RNA

Sloan and colleagues found that 5S rRNA, RPL5 and RPL11 contributed to basal p53 levels and p53 induction during experimentally impaired ribosome production in U2OS cells. This identified a ribonucleoprotein complex rather than an isolated L11 signal. [sloan2013]

Interaction, crosslinking and fractionation observations in human-cell models supported association of the 5S complex with MDM2. Localization observations showed nucleoplasmic accumulation of the complex when ribosome production was inhibited. [sloan2013]

These results connect ribosome assembly with regulation of p53 abundance. RNA depletion, tagged-protein observations and induced stress remain model-specific, and complex localization does not directly measure the amount of nucleolar damage. [sloan2013]

A focused stress response

This evidence supports one defined nuclear-stress relationship: ribosome-biogenesis disruption can engage MDM2/p53 regulation through ribosomal proteins and 5S RNA. It does not establish a single pathway governing every heat, oxidative, mechanical or DNA-damage response. [zhang2003] [sloan2013]

The L11 study was inspected at primary Abstract depth; selected descriptive Results were also inspected for the 5S-RNP study. Responses in p53-deficient tissues, all quantitative controls and clinical outcomes were not appraised. [zhang2003] [sloan2013]
