Remodeler binding is position- and context-dependent

De Dieuleveult et al. mapped nucleosomes associated with eight remodelers in mouse embryonic stem cells. Several remodelers occupied overlapping genomic regions, but their profiles at individual promoters differed with nucleosome position and promoter architecture. Chd1 was concentrated near gene starts, whereas Chd2 extended across transcription units and correlated with H3K36me3. Shared occupancy therefore did not imply identical nucleosome targeting or biological function. [1]

One remodeler can have different transcriptional effects

In the same mouse stem-cell study, depletion comparisons connected Chd4 and Ep400 to activation at many H3K4me3-only promoters but repression at many bivalent promoters. Brg1 also showed promoter-class-dependent effects. These were trends among the studied genes, not rules satisfied by every locus. At bivalent promoters, Brg1 depletion reduced accessibility while Chd4 depletion increased it, illustrating that remodelers can have opposing effects in a particular chromatin context. [1]

Acute BAF loss reveals a time-dependent accessibility response

Schick et al. followed loss of the BAF ATPase subunit SMARCA4 in human HAP1 cells. Most responsive regions lost accessibility, particularly BAF-bound enhancer regions, while relatively few gained it. Different regions responded at different rates. The tagged comparison clone already had reduced baseline SMARCA4 expression and some accessibility and transcription differences, a limitation the authors reported and addressed with control comparisons. [2]

Histone H3K27 acetylation and expression changes frequently lagged behind accessibility changes. Early nascent-transcription measurements showed relatively few significant changes. Accessibility, a histone mark and transcription were therefore related but temporally distinct readouts in this experiment; a change in one should not be reported as an immediate identical change in all three. [2]

ATPase activity and paralog context affect the response

Dual SMARCA4/SMARCA2 ATPase inhibition produced overlapping accessibility changes with SMARCA4 loss, but with different kinetics and additional affected regions. Selected combined-subunit comparisons exposed enhancer dependencies that a single-subunit perturbation did not. The authors interpreted the rapid response as evidence that ongoing BAF activity helps maintain accessibility at its controlled regions. This does not establish the same requirement at every promoter, enhancer or remodeler family. [2]

Occupancy and perturbation answer complementary questions

Nucleosome-associated binding profiles identify where remodelers are detected; perturbation time courses test how selected readouts change when a regulator is lost or its activity is suppressed. The two primary studies support remodeler- and locus-dependent regulation rather than one universal activating motor outcome. They do not by themselves establish every atomic translocation step, a complete family taxonomy, genome-repair mechanism or clinical benefit. [1] [2]
