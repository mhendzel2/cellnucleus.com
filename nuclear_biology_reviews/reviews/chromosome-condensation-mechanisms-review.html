A measured change in chromosome organization

Gibcus et al. followed synchronized chicken DT40 cells through mitosis with imaging and Hi-C. Interphase compartments and domain patterns weakened as chromosomes acquired mitotic contact patterns. Condensin depletion altered this transition. The comparison concerns population contact frequencies in a cultured vertebrate system; it does not describe a fixed conformation shared by every chromosome in every cell. [1]

Compaction and chromosome individualization are different

After depletion of the shared condensin subunit SMC2, the same study observed compacted mitotic chromatin without normal individualized, rod-shaped chromosomes. Some interphase contact features persisted. Thus, a visibly compact chromatin mass and a normally organized mitotic chromosome are different outcomes. The result does not identify every mechanism that drives compaction in condensin-depleted cells. [1]

The two condensin complexes behave differently

Walther et al. quantified fluorescently labeled endogenous condensin subunits in human HeLa Kyoto cells using calibrated live imaging and super-resolution microscopy. Condensin II was more stably associated with mitotic chromosomes and occupied a more central region of the chromatid. Condensin I associated in additional waves during prometaphase and anaphase and extended farther from the center. These measurements support different spatial and temporal roles for the two complexes in this cell line. [2]

Loop architecture is an interpretation of the measurements

Walther et al. used their abundance and localization measurements to propose a hierarchical loop model. They explicitly treated its DNA path and loop arrangement as hypothetical. The inferred loop sizes depend on assumptions that condensin complexes define loop bases and that the loops are fully extruded. Protein localization therefore supports a testable architectural model; it is not direct visualization of every DNA loop in an intact mitotic chromosome. [2]

Local nucleosome packing answers a different question

Nishino et al. examined isolated mitotic HeLa chromosomes by cryo-electron microscopy of vitreous sections and X-ray scattering. They found predominantly irregular nucleosome-fiber packing, without evidence for a dominant regular 30-nm structure in those preparations. A prominent initial scattering signal near 30 nm was associated with ribosome aggregates. Chicken erythrocyte nuclei supplied a positive control for ordered fibers. Extraction, washing and sectioning limit extrapolation to intact cells and do not exclude every minority or specialized ordered arrangement. [3]

Local nucleosome packing and chromosome-scale organization are different observables. Irregular packing in an isolated chromosome preparation can coexist with condensin-dependent chromosome organization. These studies do not establish a mandatory hierarchy of regular fibers, a universal chromosome loop size or one complete mechanism for all mitotic and meiotic compaction. [1] [2] [3]
