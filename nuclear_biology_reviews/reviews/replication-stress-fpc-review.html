Checkpoint slowing differs from failed replication

Unsal-Kaçmaz and colleagues used DNA-fiber observations in HeLa cells to distinguish replication initiation from chain elongation after DNA damage. Timeless and Tipin contributed to inhibition of new initiation, but their depletion affected elongation differently. [unsal2007]

Timeless depletion slowed progression in undamaged cells, whereas Tipin depletion had little baseline effect and diminished damage-associated slowing. The findings support both replication maintenance and a checkpoint response; slower progression is not automatically evidence of a collapsed fork. [unsal2007]

Coordination with chromosome cohesion

Leman and colleagues reported Timeless association with replication-origin regions in human cells and loss of that association as replication progressed. Timeless-Tipin-deficient cells showed chromosome fragmentation and damage-repair defects under fork stress. [leman2010]

The same study found sister-chromatid-cohesion and mitotic defects, together with Timeless-Tipin/cohesin copurification and reduced stable cohesin association with S-phase chromatin. These results support coordination between replication and cohesion in the tested cells. [leman2010]

Copurification identifies an association, not necessarily direct contact among every component. Origin occupancy, fiber progression and cohesion are separate readouts, so one does not substitute for the others. [leman2010] [unsal2007]

Scope of the fork-protection interpretation

Together, the studies support a human Timeless-Tipin role in maintaining replication and coordinating its stress response with chromosome organization. They do not establish an identical fork-protection-complex composition in every organism or a universal disease mechanism. [unsal2007] [leman2010]

Primary Abstracts and citation records were inspected. Detailed experimental controls, all physical contacts and exact cell lines in the cohesion study were not independently established at this depth. [unsal2007] [leman2010]
