Nucleolus: Structure and RNA Organization

Primary evidence on nucleolar phase coexistence, NPM1 interactions and human pre-rRNA sorting, with study-specific limits.

Nucleolar organization: study systems matter

This revised overview appraises selected primary evidence for nucleolar RNA/protein organization. It separates human cellular observations, Xenopus oocyte observations and purified mixtures. The associated Word document has broader coverage and is not appraised here.

Yao et al. (2019) studied the organization of fibrillar centers and dense fibrillar components in human nucleoli, relating nascent pre-rRNA to fibrillarin-associated organization. Their reported sorting and processing account is specific to those nucleolar structures; it is not a survey of all nucleolar activities.

Compartment architecture and coexisting phases

Feric et al. (2016) combined Xenopus oocyte nucleolar observations with reconstitution of NPM1-rich and fibrillarin-rich condensed phases in an RNA-containing mixture. Two distinct protein-rich phases coexist with surrounding solution in the reconstituted system. Those phases must not be counted as a recreation of all three anatomical nucleolar compartments.

The same study found distinct component dynamics and material behaviour in the tested nucleolar systems. This supports internal material diversity rather than a single uniform exchange rate for a whole nucleolus. Molecular fluorescence recovery and shape/material observations address different observables.

NPM1: multivalent protein/RNA interactions

Mitrea et al. (2016) linked NPM1 interaction architecture with proteins containing R-rich motifs and rRNA to droplet formation in purified mixtures. Their mammalian nucleolar localization comparisons connect those interactions to localization, providing evidence across reconstituted and cellular contexts.

Changing NPM1 interaction architecture affects binding as well as assembly. The localization evidence therefore does not isolate a phase-separation-only cause of nucleolar function. The result supports interaction-dependent organization while leaving that causal distinction open.

Nascent RNA sorting and the limits of causal language

Yao et al. (2019) reported that nascent pre-rRNA associates with fibrillarin and attributed local fibrillarin clustering and dense-fibrillar-component assembly to multivalent interactions and phase behaviour. Their interpretation links RNA sorting to compartment organization. This overview retains that descriptive result and attributed mechanism without importing the paper's quantitative counts or detailed procedures.

Together, these studies support RNA/protein-dependent organization, component-specific material behaviour and phase coexistence in selected nucleolar systems. They do not establish an identical mechanism across all human tissues, all nucleolar compartments or every condition.

Evidence context and limits

Mitrea et al: Recombinant NPM1 domains/full-length protein and partners; isolated mammalian nucleoli and mouse fibroblast localization models. Binding/structural measurements, droplet/composition imaging and localization comparisons. Purified mixture assembly and mammalian localization are complementary evidence; interaction changes are not selective phase-only changes.

Feric et al: Xenopus oocyte nucleoli observed with fluorescent protein markers, recombinant nucleolar proteins/RNA, and cellular component-dynamics comparisons. Fusion/shape relaxation, fluorescence recovery, microrheology, composition-dependent coexistence and component localization. Xenopus oocyte and reconstituted systems; two condensed phases plus solution are not all three anatomical nucleolar compartments.

Yao et al: Human nucleoli; retained claims stay at Abstract-supported cellular scope because precise cell-line and expression context were not independently appraised. Descriptive cellular ultrastructure, protein/RNA organization and nascent pre-rRNA sorting observations. Abstract-depth appraisal; no detailed quantitative architecture, cell-line-specific inference, methods validity or phase-only causal isolation claimed.
