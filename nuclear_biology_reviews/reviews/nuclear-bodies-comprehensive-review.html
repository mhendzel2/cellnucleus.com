Comparing bodies through primary evidence

This overview compares selected evidence for nucleoli, speckles, paraspeckles, Cajal bodies and PML bodies. Their assembly, molecular composition, material behaviour and biological effects are separate questions. Defined human/mouse cellular examples are kept distinct from Xenopus oocyte observations and purified mixtures.

Context before generalization

A result about a purified protein/RNA mixture is evidence that those components can organize collectively under the studied conditions. It is not the same as demonstrating the dominant mechanism of a body in an intact human or mouse cell. The nucleolar studies of Mitrea et al. (2016) and Feric et al. (2016) provide both reconstituted and cellular observations, whose scopes must be described separately.

What fluorescence and composition establish

Banani et al. (2016) measured selective client recruitment within phase-separated model scaffolds. Recruitment depended on cognate interactions, scaffold composition and valency. Selective binding and phase behaviour can therefore coexist; neither selective enrichment nor a fluorescent focus alone establishes the mechanism of every endogenous body.

Component recovery and phase coexistence address different observables. Feric et al. (2016) and Banani et al. (2016) found component-specific dynamics alongside compositional evidence. A single recovery curve should therefore not be treated as a universal material-state diagnosis.

Assembly mechanisms need body-specific evidence

NEAT1-dependent paraspeckle integrity, SON/SRRM2-dependent speckle organization and oxidation-associated PML assembly are distinct findings in the cited models. They do not establish that all bodies use one scaffold chemistry or one thermodynamic mechanism.

Selected evidence for five bodies

Feric et al. (2016) observed nucleolar component organization in Xenopus oocytes and reconstituted two protein-rich phases plus surrounding solution. Mitrea et al. (2016) connected NPM1/protein/RNA interactions to purified-mixture assembly and mammalian nucleolar localization. These observations support specific RNA/protein organization rather than a complete reconstruction of every nucleolar compartment.

Ilik et al. (2020) found that loss of both SON and SRRM2 strongly dispersed speckle organization in tested cell models, and corrected the SC35 monoclonal antibody's principal target to SRRM2. Assembly dependence and marker identity should be kept separate from claims about every RNA-processing function.

Clemson et al. (2009) linked NEAT1 RNA to paraspeckle organization in tested cellular models, with loss of visible paraspeckles accompanying NEAT1 loss. This supports an architectural role for that RNA in those models; it does not establish a universal gene-retention function or liquid-phase mechanism.

Darzacq et al. (2002) identified human small Cajal body-specific RNAs associated with guide functions in spliceosomal snRNA modification. The paper distinguishes predicted from demonstrated guide-RNA functions. Localization and those selected functions do not prove that every modification requires a visible Cajal body.

Sahin et al. (2014) distinguished oxidation-associated initial PML mesh assembly from SUMO/SIM-associated partner recruitment and retention in cell models. Assembly, partner composition and exchange should not be merged into one all-liquid-body description.

RNA-processing effects require defined scope

Małszycki et al. (2026) reported inefficient splicing and reduced expression of GC-rich genes with short introns after acute loss of SON/SRRM2 in human cells. This supports a restricted processing dependence; direct functions of the removed proteins and spatial architecture remain coupled. The result does not extend equally to all transcripts or all nuclear bodies.

Disease-wide, therapeutic and pathogen/viral applications are outside this overview. The associated Word review retains broader topics, but its scientific text and references are not covered by this HTML appraisal.

Evidence limits

The cited studies provide different forms of evidence: component localization, assembly dependence, RNA/protein association, composition-dependent coexistence and particular RNA-processing phenotypes. These observables support the described findings, while conclusions about universal mechanisms or architecture-only function would need additional system-specific evidence.

Evidence assessment

The checked examples show multiple routes to nuclear organization and selected functional connections. They support treating each body, component and biological system explicitly. They do not establish one shared material state, one universal scaffold or one function for every nuclear body.

Evidence context and limits

Mitrea et al: Recombinant NPM1 domains/full-length protein and partners; isolated mammalian nucleoli and mouse fibroblast localization models. Binding/structural measurements, droplet/composition imaging and localization comparisons. Purified mixture assembly and mammalian localization are complementary evidence; interaction changes are not selective phase-only changes.

Feric et al: Xenopus oocyte nucleoli observed with fluorescent protein markers, recombinant nucleolar proteins/RNA, and cellular component-dynamics comparisons. Fusion/shape relaxation, fluorescence recovery, microrheology, composition-dependent coexistence and component localization. Xenopus oocyte and reconstituted systems; two condensed phases plus solution are not all three anatomical nucleolar compartments.

Sahin et al: Mouse fibroblast and human-cell PML models, with PML/SUMO interaction comparisons. Cell imaging, biochemical assembly comparisons, partner localization and retention. Tested cell models distinguish initial PML assembly from partner recruitment/retention; no whole-body liquid phase-coexistence diagnosis.

Ilik et al: Cultured human-cell speckle models; current Abstract identity/result check supplemented by shared prior descriptive primary Results profile. Speckle-marker distributions and SON/SRRM2-associated assembly comparisons. Assembly-marker redistribution is not a selective removal of only geometry; direct protein effects can remain.

Małszycki et al: Human cells with acute SON/SRRM2 loss; precise engineered line details not independently appraised here. Descriptive expression and RNA-splicing comparisons reported in the primary Abstract. Abstract depth; no timing/quantitative effect size/method controls or isolated architectural causality validated.

Clemson et al: Cellular paraspeckle models; precise cell-line identity not specified in the inspected Abstract. Descriptive RNA/protein colocalization, RNA association and body-integrity comparisons. RNA-dependent body integrity does not establish every RNA-retention function or a universal phase mechanism.

Darzacq et al: Human small Cajal body-specific RNAs; no universal species/body-composition claim retained. Descriptive RNA localization and guide-RNA-function evidence summarized in the primary Abstract. Predicted guide targets are distinguished from demonstrated functions; visible-body necessity is not established for every snRNA modification.

Banani et al: Purified model scaffold/client droplets; not an endogenous diagnosis of every nuclear body. Client partitioning, scaffold composition and component-recovery comparisons. Selectivity in model phases establishes compatibility with demixing, not its prevalence across all endogenous bodies.
