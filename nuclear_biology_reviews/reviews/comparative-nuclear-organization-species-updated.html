Human fibroblasts: chromosome positions, not an evolutionary template

Bolzer and colleagues used multicolor 3D DNA FISH in fixed diploid human male fibroblasts. Their quiescent-cell maps showed probabilistic chromosome-size-related radial ordering and variable chromosome neighbors. The principal fibroblast radial measurements did not show a significant chromosome 18/19 distinction. Nuclear geometry and the chosen distance measurement matter; these findings cannot supply a universal vertebrate chromosome address system. Bolzer et al., 2005.

Budding yeast: genome-wide contacts and constrained reconstruction

In cultured haploid Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Duan and colleagues used a 3C-derived sequencing assay to estimate genome-wide contact frequencies. Enriched centromere contacts, chromosome-arm constraints and chromosome XII organization around the rDNA region supported a Rabl-consistent arrangement. The model combined these population contacts with distance and nuclear-landmark assumptions. It is a plausible ensemble reconstruction, not a direct movie of one yeast nucleus. Duan et al., 2010.

Mouse embryos: developmental state changes within one species

Ahmed and colleagues correlated lineage-marker fluorescence with electron spectroscopic imaging of mouse embryo sections. At the blastocyst stage, epiblast chromatin was more dispersed than chromatin in trophectoderm and primitive-endoderm cells. Later epiblast cells showed more compact domains. Corresponding cultured stem-cell populations resembled the examined embryonic counterparts. These within-species contrasts show why “the mouse nucleus” is too broad a category for a single structural description. Ahmed et al., 2010.

How to compare the evidence without confounding the assay

The human experiment measures painted chromosome geometry, the yeast experiment measures population ligation contacts, and the mouse experiment resolves local ultrastructure and elemental distributions. Differences between those readouts cannot be assigned to species alone. A controlled comparison would need matched cell states, equivalent observable features and comparable analysis. These three studies do not reconstruct an ancestral nucleus, demonstrate a universal compartment hierarchy, or establish a linear rise in nuclear complexity. They offer specific measurements that can guide such questions. Bolzer et al., 2005; Duan et al., 2010; Ahmed et al., 2010.
