Overview

Architecture can be studied by combining morphology with molecular labels. The selected examples are paraspeckle RNA-region localization by electron microscopy, PML shell/core labeling and nucleolar-component imaging with material assays. These methods return different evidence and do not establish one assembly mechanism for all bodies. Souquere et al. (2010); Sahin et al. (2014); Feric et al. (2016).

1. Molecular identity matters

Souquere et al. combined paraspeckle protein markers with electron microscopy and in-situ hybridization of NEAT1 regions. The markers connected the observed electron-dense structures with the paraspeckle identity in tested human/mouse cells. An unlabeled shape alone would not establish that molecular identity. Souquere et al. (2010).

2. What prepared-section localization can show

The paraspeckle study examined fixed embedded sections, immunogold protein labeling and RNA hybridization. NEAT1 terminal regions were preferentially peripheral, whereas internal long-transcript regions were central; assay controls supported the distinct distributions. This is spatial evidence in prepared material, not a live exchange-rate or phase-diagram measurement. Souquere et al. (2010).

3. Two distinct architectural examples

In HeLa and NIH3T3 cells, the Souquere study found elongated paraspeckles and a constrained short dimension. The RNA-region distribution supported ordered organization, while cross-wise versus looped single-transcript placement was not uniquely resolved; the authors favored a looped model. Souquere et al. (2010).

Sahin et al. used immunoelectron microscopy in CHO models and confocal/SIM or biochemical comparisons in other tested cells to distinguish a PML-rich outer shell from a partner-enriched inner core. The distribution concerns the tested models and labels, not every PML body under every condition. Sahin et al. (2014).

4. Architecture does not uniquely diagnose a phase

Feric et al. combined Xenopus nucleolar imaging with purified NPM1/FIB1-RNA droplet fusion, recovery, microrheology and wetting observations. Their distinct phase/material interpretation relies on that combined evidence rather than a single electron-microscopy appearance. Feric et al. (2016).

Paraspeckle RNA topology and PML shell/core labeling describe molecular organization, but those spatial measurements do not independently establish equilibrium liquid coexistence or one universal condensate mechanism. Souquere et al. (2010); Sahin et al. (2014).

5. Functional evidence is a separate readout

Sahin et al. examined partner sumoylation and its association with PML-body assembly in specified cell/stress comparisons. That biochemical readout provides more than morphology alone, but does not show that every inner-core geometry optimizes every nuclear function. Sahin et al. (2014).

Feric et al. propose a link between layered nucleolar material properties and ribosomal processing. The proposal should remain distinct from a direct causal test that changing architecture alone optimizes ribosome production. Feric et al. (2016).

6. Stress observations do not establish all disease mechanisms

Sahin et al. found oxidative-stress-associated PML assembly and altered partner modification in defined cell/in-vivo comparisons. This supports a specific stress response, not a universal pathological ultrastructure, diagnostic marker or clinical treatment conclusion. Sahin et al. (2014).

7. Compare modalities and specimens

The selected evidence spans prepared thin-section RNA/protein localization, PML labeling/biochemistry and live/reconstituted nucleolar material assays. Preparation, molecular-label identity and the measured observable should remain explicit before structures are compared or assigned a physical mechanism. Souquere et al. (2010); Sahin et al. (2014); Feric et al. (2016).
