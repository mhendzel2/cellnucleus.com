1. Scope: distinguish structure and function

Cryo-electron tomography resolves a filament network in prepared mouse fibroblast nuclei, genome mapping identifies lamina-associated domains in human fibroblasts, and cell-strain experiments reveal mechanical/signaling phenotypes of lamin-A/C loss. These are complementary evidence types rather than one experiment proving every proposed lamina function. Turgay et al. (2017); Guelen et al. (2008); Lammerding et al. (2004).

2. Filament architecture in a defined preparation

Turgay et al. used extracted, chromatin-reduced vimentin-null mouse embryonic fibroblasts for cryo-electron tomography and lamin A/C or B1 immunogold labeling. They resolved approximately 3.5-nanometre filaments arranged as a meshwork in the examined lamina. That dimension concerns the prepared filaments, not an invariant thickness of all live-cell laminae. Turgay et al. (2017).

The structural study describes variable filament packing and uses modeling to interpret molecular organization. Static structural assignments do not themselves establish real-time assembly dynamics, force transmission or the function of every interacting protein. Turgay et al. (2017).

3. Functional links without an exhaustive interactome

Lamin-A/C-null mouse fibroblasts deformed more under strain and showed altered selected gene responses. NF-kB transcriptional output was reduced despite increased binding in the tested stimulation context. This demonstrates functional consequences of lamin loss without identifying every molecular partner or proving a complete transcriptional scaffold mechanism. Lammerding et al. (2004).

In isolated-nucleus pulling, lamin A/C primarily influenced the large-extension response, while chromatin affected the short-extension response. The roles depend on the tested geometry and preparation; this is not a universal statement that lamins alone determine nuclear mechanics. Stephens et al. (2017).

4. Lamina-associated domains are an association map

In the inspected primary abstract, Guelen et al. report a genome-wide human-fibroblast map with more than 1,300 lamina-associated domains, spanning 0.1–10 megabases and characterized by low gene expression. Those numbers and associations are explicit in the primary abstract; full protocols and locus-level data were not appraised. Guelen et al. (2008).

The inspected primary abstract describes boundary associations with CTCF, outward-oriented promoters or CpG islands and proposes possible confinement mechanisms. These observations do not establish permanent tethers or prove that lamina contact causes repression at every locus. Guelen et al. (2008).

An indexed erratum is listed in the original PubMed record: Nature. 2013 Aug 8;500(7461):242. The saved record supplies no correction DOI or PMID, and the correction’s full text was not appraised. Its identifiers, affected material and impact on the reported lamina-domain findings remain unverified here; the observations above are attributed to the inspected primary abstract.

5. Two bounded human genetic examples

Bonne et al. identified four LMNA variants co-segregating with autosomal-dominant Emery-Dreifuss muscular dystrophy in five families. Eriksson et al. reported recurrent G608G in 18 of 20 classical HGPS cases, cryptic splicing and a 50-amino-acid deletion in the resulting protein. These are primary-abstract findings, not a comprehensive catalogue of laminopathy variants. Bonne et al. (1999); Eriksson et al. (2003).

Eriksson et al. also describe abnormal nuclear membranes in HGPS fibroblasts. This specific genotype/phenotype evidence does not by itself establish identical changes for other LMNA-associated disorders or normal human aging. Eriksson et al. (2003).

6. Mechanical and signaling mechanisms remain bounded

The lamin-A/C-null mouse-fibroblast study found both defective nuclear mechanics and altered mechanically activated transcription. Its discussion treats the relation to tissue-specific human laminopathies as a proposed explanation; strain-associated viability loss was not explained solely by observed rupture. No unified mechanism for all variants is established here. Lammerding et al. (2004).

7. Senescence association differs from general aging

Freund et al. found lower lamin-B1 in several primary human fibroblast senescence conditions, while quiescent controls retained it. The study also examined irradiated versus control mouse liver. These are defined culture and induced-tissue comparisons, not proof that lamin-B1 loss is universal in ordinary human aging. Freund et al. (2012).

In the mouse liver comparison, lamin-B1 intensity distributions overlapped sufficiently that individual nuclei could not be confidently classified by that signal alone. The descriptive senescence association therefore is not a validated clinical diagnostic or a lifespan outcome. Freund et al. (2012).

8. Conclusions and review limits

The retained evidence establishes a prepared filament meshwork, mapped genome-lamina associations, defined mechanical/signaling phenotypes and specific genetic or senescence-associated observations. Moving between these scales requires additional causal evidence; no exhaustive interactome, universal disease mechanism or clinical effectiveness follows from this selected review. Turgay et al. (2017); Guelen et al. (2008); Lammerding et al. (2004); Freund et al. (2012).
