Parental and newly deposited histones differ

Alabert et al. used nascent-chromatin capture with isotope labeling and mass spectrometry to distinguish parental from newly synthesized histones in cultured human somatic cells. Recycled parental histones carried a variety of methylation marks, whereas newly deposited histones had a different modification pattern, including prominent H4 acetylation. Histone incorporation after replication therefore did not produce one chemically uniform population. [1]

Maintenance does not require instant identical copying

For many measured modifications, new histones gradually acquired a pattern resembling parental histones. H3K27me3 and H3K9me3 behaved differently: both old and new histones continued to acquire methylation, and these marks accumulated with histone age. Restoration of the total mark level and completion of modification on each new histone are thus different outcomes. This result is specific to the assayed populations and should not be described as a universal clock for all histone PTMs. [1]

Regional occupancy supplies a complementary view

In mouse embryonic stem cells, Liu et al. followed H3K27me3 occupancy on newly replicated DNA. Regions enriched for repressive marks and H1e restored more rapidly than other sampled regions. Partial loss of H1c/d/e reduced H3K27me3 and delayed its restoration. This regional measurement complements histone-age measurements, but does not map a unique modified nucleosome conformation or make chromatin accessibility equivalent to transcription. [2]

Histone acetylation and regulator function can diverge

You et al. studied mice with altered deacetylase-activation domains in the NCoR and SMRT corepressors. Endogenous HDAC3 activity was undetectable in the tested assays despite persistence of the HDAC3 protein. At selected liver sites, H3K9 and H3K27 acetylation increased. Nevertheless, these mice did not reproduce the full liver phenotype or expression changes caused by removing HDAC3 protein. The authors retained the possibility of residual activity below assay detection. [3]

This comparison cautions against treating one acetylation readout as a complete description of regulator function. A chromatin mark, the abundance and occupancy of its regulators, and a gene-expression change are related measurements with distinct causal interpretations. The checked methylation and acetylation studies support retaining that context rather than assigning each modification a single universal activation or repression outcome. [1] [2] [3]
