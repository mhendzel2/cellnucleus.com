What a visible replication site measures

Leonhardt and colleagues followed GFP-PCNA in living mouse C2C12 cells. Colocalization with replication proteins and nucleotide incorporation supported using these foci as markers of active DNA synthesis. [leonhardt2000]

The foci changed distribution during S phase through gradual appearance and disappearance. The recorded sites showed little directional movement, supporting local assembly and disassembly rather than relocation of one persistent factory. [leonhardt2000]

The authors interpreted positional stability as anchoring. Imaging established relative position; it did not identify a physical nuclear anchor or prove that every focus contains a fixed number of replication units. [leonhardt2000]

Resolution changes the unit that is counted

Chagin and colleagues compared conventional and super-resolution imaging in human HeLa Kyoto and mouse C2C12 cells. Structures appearing as single conventional foci separated into multiple smaller sites at higher resolution. [chagin2016]

Combining site counts with genome size, S-phase duration and DNA-fiber measurements supported resolving many sites to individual-replicon scale. This assignment was an integrated inference, rather than direct identification of each origin from an image. [chagin2016]

Late-S clustering, optical resolution and non-diploid cell models limit a universal one-focus/one-replicon rule. The two studies therefore illustrate both local replication-site dynamics and the dependence of a visible factory on the scale of observation. [leonhardt2000] [chagin2016]

Access and biological limits

Primary Abstracts, selected descriptive Results and citation identities were inspected. These cultured-cell observations do not establish identical site kinetics in every tissue, a complete replication-timing programme or a structurally defined nuclear scaffold. [leonhardt2000] [chagin2016]
