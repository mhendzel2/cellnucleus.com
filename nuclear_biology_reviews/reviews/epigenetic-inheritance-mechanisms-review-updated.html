A measured transmission problem at DNA replication

A concrete question is how histone modifications persist while newly replicated DNA acquires old and new histones. Alabert and colleagues combined nascent chromatin capture, isotope labeling and mass spectrometry in cultured human cells. They recovered modified parental histones on newly replicated DNA and distinguished those peptides from newly synthesized histones. This demonstrates molecular carryover and subsequent modification dynamics at the population level; it does not by itself demonstrate inheritance of a cellular phenotype or an acquired trait across organismal generations. Alabert et al., 2015.

Restoring total marks differs from copying each old histone

New histone deposition diluted modification levels in the recovered chromatin, after which many new-histone profiles approached parental profiles. H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 followed a different pattern: modification continued on both parental and new histones, and histone age influenced their abundance. Total recovery can therefore coexist with unequal profiles on old and new histones. These data do not justify a universal immediate “copy and paste” mechanism from each parental nucleosome to its new neighbor. Alabert et al., 2015.

Regional recovery and an H1 contribution

Liu and colleagues added genomic context by following H3K27me3 recovery on replicated DNA in mouse embryonic stem cells. ChOR-seq showed different regional recovery behavior. Selected H1 depletion delayed recovery in chronic knockout and inducible comparisons. The data support an H1 contribution in those cells, but the sequencing readout is an ensemble association of a mark with nascent chromatin. It does not directly trace every parental histone, isolate compaction from every other H1 function, or prove that mark recovery alone preserves cell identity. Liu et al., 2023.

Molecular turnover and what a broader inheritance claim needs

Deal and colleagues found measurable histone turnover even at Polycomb-associated sites in Drosophila S2 cells. Molecular replacement therefore must be distinguished from persistence of a broader regulatory state. Evidence for a histone-mark mechanism requires knowing what is maintained, where and over what interval; evidence for an inherited trait additionally needs a measured phenotype and an appropriate transmission design. The experiments reviewed here concern cultured-cell incorporation and replication-associated mark recovery. They do not establish human transgenerational inheritance, environmental inheritance, germline exceptions or a general hierarchy of DNA methylation, RNA and histone memory mechanisms. Deal et al., 2010; Alabert et al., 2015; Liu et al., 2023.
