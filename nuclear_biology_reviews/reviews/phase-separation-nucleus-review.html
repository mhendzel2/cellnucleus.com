Model principles with a defined biological reach

This overview introduces demixing, selective composition, network connectivity and polymer organization through five primary studies. It separates theory and purified models from observations of particular nuclear bodies. It does not assume that every nuclear compartment is a liquid droplet.

Demixing in defined interaction mixtures

Li et al. (2012) found concentration- and valency-dependent phase behaviour in purified multivalent interaction mixtures. Here, demixing refers to the formation of compositionally distinct condensed and surrounding regions in such a tested mixture. This is a collective property of that mixture, rather than a synonym for any molecular interaction or visible cluster.

Banani et al. (2016) showed that clients sort into model phase-separated scaffolds through cognate interactions, with recruitment depending on scaffold composition and valency. Specific binding and compositionally distinct phases are compatible. The observation contradicts a definition of LLPS that requires nonspecific recruitment, without proving that every selectively enriched nuclear focus is phase separated.

Network connectivity is a separate question

Harmon et al. (2017) used coarse-grained simulations and associative-polymer theory to distinguish demixing from gelation, described through formation of system-spanning molecular networks. Their model allows network formation with or without demixing. Gelation and concentration separation therefore are related but distinct in this theoretical treatment.

The same study analysed how the physical properties of disordered linkers influence the coupling between network formation and phase separation in linear multivalent proteins. This provides a model of linker-dependent assembly. It does not establish a universal sequence rule or an experimentally measured phase boundary for every disordered nuclear protein.

Polymer organization without an assumed liquid diagnosis

Brackley et al. (2013) simulated multivalent proteins binding DNA/chromatin polymers and obtained clusters without an explicitly attractive protein-protein potential. This demonstrates a possible bridging-associated mechanism within the model. It should not be treated as a direct measurement of liquid coexistence or as the mechanism of every endogenous focus.

Internal material diversity in a nuclear-body example

Feric et al. (2016) combined Xenopus oocyte nucleolar observations with reconstitution containing NPM1, fibrillarin and RNA. Two protein-rich condensed phases and surrounding solution had distinct component/material behaviour. This is an example of internal material diversity, not a reconstruction of all three anatomical nucleolar compartments or a single state assigned to every component.

Demixing, network connectivity and recovery of a labelled component ask different questions. The distinct component behaviour in Banani et al. (2016) and Feric et al. (2016) illustrates why a recovery curve or rounded shape alone cannot serve as a universal diagnosis of the complete assembly mechanism.

Evidence assessment and theory boundary

These studies support context-specific demixing, selective sorting, network coupling, polymer clustering and internal material diversity. Comparing them requires retaining their molecular and biological settings. This overview omits the legacy classical polymer-solution equation and derivation because its original primary source was not accessible during this appraisal; it does not claim complete coverage of phase-transition theory.

Evidence context and limits

Li et al: Engineered multivalent SH3 and proline-rich-motif proteins and PTB/RNA reconstitution; valency-dependent phase diagrams; droplet/bulk partitioning; dynamic light scattering, SAXS, photobleaching and cryo-EM; additional HeLa puncta and nephrin/Nck/N-WASP reconstitution context. Descriptive primary observations/modelling at the recorded source depth; no operational procedures reproduced. Sol–gel coupling is the authors’ qualified interpretation, not a directly measured universal percolation threshold or nuclear material state. The page preserves likely and confines claims to reconstituted systems.

Banani et al: Purified engineered polySUMO/polySIM, polySH3/polyPRM, and PTB/polyUCUCU scaffold pairs with fluorescent low-valency clients; phase diagrams, partition coefficients, binding mutants, valency series, and mass-action modeling. Current page claims stay with these engineered systems. Descriptive primary observations/modelling at the recorded source depth; no operational procedures reproduced. SUMO/SIM mutant/GFP controls are not described as controls for every scaffold pair. Does not show all endogenous bodies demix or assign a phase mechanism solely from enrichment.

Harmon et al: Coarse-grained simulations and associative-polymer theory for linear multivalent proteins with disordered linkers. The Abstract distinguishes demixing from formation of system-spanning networks and describes linker-dependent coupling. A theoretical/model result; no measured endogenous-body threshold or universal biological preference established here.

Brackley et al: LAMMPS Brownian-dynamics bead-and-spring DNA/chromatin polymers with reversibly binding model proteins. Protein–protein and template–template interactions have steric repulsion only. Bridging, monovalent controls, protein-size variation, and chromatin simulations informed by Pol II/NFkB ChIP-seq; comparisons with experimental contact data. Descriptive primary observations/modelling at the recorded source depth; no operational procedures reproduced. Finite time, chosen coarse-graining/interactions, and starting conformations limit in-cell inference. Chromosome territory behavior depends on initial conditions and some simulated cluster organization differs from cells. This is simulation evidence, not diagnosis of a named endogenous focus.

Feric et al: Amplified extrachromosomal nucleoli in X. laevis oocytes, fluorescent subcompartment markers, actin perturbation to permit fusion; purified NPM1/FIB1/rRNA mixtures; FRAP and embedded 50-nm probe microrheology; ATP depletion in oocyte and mammalian-cell conditions. Descriptive primary observations/modelling at the recorded source depth; no operational procedures reproduced. Two protein-rich phases are not a reconstitution of all three nucleolar compartments or a universal nuclear-body model. Aging and ATP observations are component/condition-specific; no standalone FRAP-to-solid-state inference.
