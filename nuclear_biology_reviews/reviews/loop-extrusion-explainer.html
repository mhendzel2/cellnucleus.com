Loop Extrusion Model

Scientists study how proteins gather DNA into loops by comparing computer models, cell experiments and movies of purified molecules.

The idea: gather DNA into a growing loop

Imagine gathering more of a long ribbon into a loop. Loop extrusion is a similar proposal for DNA and a protein complex. Computer models using this rule reproduced some patterns in DNA contact maps. That agreement makes the idea useful, but a computer model is not a recording of a cell. [1]

One test removes the protein

In engineered human cancer cells grown in the laboratory, researchers removed a component of cohesin. The DNA contact map lost its loop-domain patterns, while another organizational pattern remained. This shows that cohesin mattered for those domains in those cells. The map combines many cells; it does not show every DNA loop moving. [2]

Another test watches molecules

Researchers also watched loops grow on DNA with purified human proteins and ATP, a chemical energy source. A protein called CTCF sometimes stopped the growing loop. Whether it stopped the loop depended on its direction and how stretched the DNA was. This is direct evidence in a simplified laboratory system, not a movie of an entire nucleus. [3]
