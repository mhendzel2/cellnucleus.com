Sequence-specific RNA probes resolve one nuclear-body architecture

Souquere and colleagues examined paraspeckles using protein-marker and RNA-hybridization electron microscopy in HeLa and NIH3T3 cells. Probes for different NEAT1 regions showed peripheral RNA ends and a long-transcript central region in the core. This assigns RNA sequences to positions within an identified body, providing more specific evidence than electron-dense morphology alone. It does not establish that every nuclear granule is a packaged export-ready messenger RNA. Souquere et al., 2010.

RNA-built geometry and a physical assembly model

Yamazaki and colleagues reported that NEAT1_2 domain changes and transcriptional modulation altered paraspeckle organization in ways interpreted by a block-copolymer micelle model. Their proposed microphase-separation framework addresses finite body shape, size and number. This is an example of RNA organization helping constrain an assembly model. It is not a direct reconstruction of every protein contact or a demonstration that a particular phase transition causes all RNA-processing functions. Yamazaki et al., 2021.

Speckle assembly depends on particular protein components

Ilik and colleagues studied SRRM2 truncations and SON depletion in human cell models, including HAP1 and independently tagged HEK293 cells. Combined disruption dispersed speckle markers. Their antibody analysis identified SRRM2 as the principal speckle target of the widely used SC35 reagent. These are component-dependence and marker-identity results. Because SON and SRRM2 also have molecular functions, their removal does not selectively manipulate geometry while leaving every RNA-processing interaction intact. Ilik et al., 2020.

A gene-association experiment supplies a separate functional readout

Kim and colleagues studied heat-shock Hsp70 loci in human HAP1 and hamster CHOK1 cells, with human HSPA1B BAC reporters in CHOK1. DNA/RNA labeling and live nascent-RNA imaging linked speckle association to increased RNA accumulation in the tested system. Accumulation is not identical to transcription initiation, and these HSPA1A/HSPA1B genes are intronless, so the experiment is not proof of improved intron removal. Connecting a body’s structure to function requires such defined assays rather than a morphology-based assembly-line story. Kim et al., 2020.
