H3K9ac associates with pathology in human cortex

Klein et al. profiled H3K9 acetylation in postmortem dorsolateral prefrontal-cortex gray matter from 669 participants in the ROS and MAP aging cohorts. Their joint statistical analysis found substantially more H3K9ac regions associated with tau burden than with amyloid-beta burden. This is an association in sampled bulk tissue across individuals; it does not establish that the histone mark initiated disease or that the same relationship holds for every brain region or cell type. [1]

Architectural associations use external annotations

Tau-associated H3K9ac changes clustered into broad genomic segments and were related to compartment and lamina annotations. The compartment reference came from fetal-brain Hi-C, and the lamina reference from a human fibrosarcoma cell line. These comparisons support an association with genomic architectural context, while leaving patient-specific three-dimensional chromosome reorganization unmeasured. The authors explicitly called for direct contact-mapping studies. [1]

A histone-mark change is not an identical transcript change

In the same study, matched and repurposed expression datasets showed some concordance with the broad epigenomic pattern, including observations in isolated neurons. At individual-transcript resolution, however, H3K9ac and expression effects were only weakly correlated, and the segment pattern accounted for a limited portion of expression variation. H3K9 acetylation therefore should not be treated as a complete measure of transcription or a universal switch for every associated gene. [1]

The neuronal model did not reproduce every tissue direction

Klein et al. also compared cultured human induced neurons with increased MAPT expression against controls and observed chromatin-accessibility and H3K9ac changes. The relation of those changes to lamina annotations had the opposite direction from the human cortical-tissue association. The model thus supports a context-dependent response to tau in those cells, while warning against presenting it as a complete reproduction of aging human brain pathology. [1]

H4K16ac supplies a different human-tissue comparison

The primary abstract of Nativio et al. describes genome-wide H4K16ac comparisons in human lateral temporal-lobe samples. According to that abstract, older cognitively normal controls showed enrichment relative to younger controls, whereas Alzheimer-disease samples showed losses near genes linked to aging and disease. This is a separately measured acetylation mark in a different cortical region and cohort; it should not be merged into a claim that all acetylation rises or falls uniformly in neurodegeneration. [2]

The indexed primary record identifies a linked correction (DOI 10.1038/s41593-018-0124-2, PMID 29556027). Its full text was not appraised. The affected material and any impact on the reported H4K16ac findings or conclusions remain unverified here; the findings above are attributed to the inspected primary abstract. Linked correction [2]

The retained evidence supports context, rather than a therapy claim

These studies support disease-associated differences in selected histone-acetylation profiles, with effects that depend on mark, genomic region, tissue and model system. They do not establish a universal histone code for neuronal degeneration, a validated biomarker for an individual patient or an effective treatment that follows from changing acetylation. Broad learning, memory, differentiation and therapeutic claims require separate primary evidence. [1] [2]
