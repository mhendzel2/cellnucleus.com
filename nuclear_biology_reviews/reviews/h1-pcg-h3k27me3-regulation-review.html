Restoration varies across chromatin regions

Liu et al. followed H3K27me3 occupancy on newly replicated DNA in mouse embryonic stem cells. The mark recovered at different rates across genomic regions; faster-restoring regions were enriched for H1e and other repressive chromatin marks. These regional associations are evidence about measured occupancy and recovery, rather than proof that every H1-rich region has the same physical structure or recovery time. [1]

H1 depletion changes the mark and its restoration

Cells lacking H1c, H1d and H1e retained other H1 subtypes but showed reduced H3K27me3, with similar measured amounts of SUZ12. H3K27me3 restoration after replication was delayed, particularly in the regions most affected by H1 loss. A separate H1c/e-depletion comparison also impaired restoration before a substantial difference in total H3K27me3 had developed. That comparison helps address unequal starting mark levels as an explanation for the restoration phenotype. [1]

These cellular results support an H1-dependent contribution to H3K27me3 maintenance. They do not directly resolve every nucleosome pair or demonstrate that a regular 30-nm fiber is required in the cell. The paper’s compaction-based interpretation should remain distinct from direct measurement of the complete endogenous DNA path. [1]

The expression response is not uniform

The same study found both increased and decreased transcript levels after partial H1 loss. The authors considered indirect effects and regulatory mechanisms beyond H3K27me3 for the decreased genes. It therefore does not support a rule that reducing H1 activates every Polycomb-associated gene, or that every expression difference is caused by loss of this one mark. [1]

Mark restoration and histone age are different measurements

Alabert et al. tracked parental and newly synthesized histones in cultured human somatic cells using nascent-chromatin capture, isotope labeling and mass spectrometry. Parental histones retained modifications during replication, while incorporation of new histones diluted the measured methylation levels. For H3K27me3 and H3K9me3, both old and new histones acquired additional methylation; new histones did not simply become identical copies of their neighbors immediately after replication. [2]

The two studies measure different scales: regional H3K27me3 occupancy in mouse stem cells and the age-dependent modification of recovered human histone populations. Together they favor context-dependent restoration over a single instant copying rule. They do not establish a family-wide H1 methylation switch, a complete PRC1/PRC2 recruitment pathway or clinical efficacy. [1] [2]
